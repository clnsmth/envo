###  Usage of 'composed primarily of' relation 
 In ENVO, we use [RO:'composed primarily of'](http://purl.obolibrary.org/obo/RO_0002473) to describe the main constituent of a material entity. This is often a relation between an [ENVO:'environmental material'](http://purl.obolibrary.org/obo/ENVO_00010483) and some [BFO:'material entity'](http://purl.obolibrary.org/obo/BFO_0000040) (including another kind of environmental material). For example, the [ENVO:pedosphere]( http://purl.obolibrary.org/obo/ENVO_01000820) would be RO:'composed primarily of' some [ENVO:soil](http://purl.obolibrary.org/obo/ENVO_00001998). ENVO:'environmental material' is a [BFO:'fiat object part'](http://purl.obolibrary.org/obo/BFO_0000024) - this means that we're dealing with portions of material (soil, water, etc) with no volume or boundary necessarily delineated by physical discontinuities. 

 We also use the RO:'composed primarily of' relation between material entities and chemical entities from CHEBI. For example, a portion of [ENVO:'liquid water'](http://purl.obolibrary.org/obo/ENVO_00002006) would be RO:'composed primarily of' some [CHEBI:'water'](http://purl.obolibrary.org/obo/CHEBI_15377). This usage does not suggest that the material is primarily composed of a single water molecule, but this is being clarified through discussion on [this issue](https://github.com/EnvironmentOntology/envo/issues/768), this should be parsed as:  "material composed primarily of things of the same class as some molecule". More formal description will be available in the annotation properties of RO:'composed primarily of' (see [this issue](https://github.com/oborel/obo-relations/issues/323))
